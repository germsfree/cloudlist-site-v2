most to-do apps assume you wake up the same person every day

so the list stays the same whether you slept nine hours or three. the task you dreaded on monday is still sitting there on thursday, in the same font, at the same size, asking the same thing.

and when you can't face it, the app has nothing to say except a number going up.

cloudlist starts somewhere else. it asks how you are first.

how it works

01 · the check-in

how's your brain today?

foggy, okay-ish, or sparking. that's the whole question. your answer decides what the app shows you for the rest of the day, and you can change it whenever things shift.

02 · now and next

only what fits, right now

tasks sort into now and next. now is what your current energy can handle. next is everything else, dimmed and waiting, not asking anything of you. and if something has been sitting untouched for days it surfaces on its own under still here, with no scolding, just a note about how long it's been.

03 · break it down

big things become small things

when a task looks too big to start, cloudlist offers to split it. you get steps small enough that beginning stops feeling like a decision. edit them, reorder them, add your own. starting is the hard part, so this makes the first step smaller.

04 · brain dump

empty your head onto the page

type everything at once. a list, a sentence, a ramble at 1am. cloudlist reads it and turns it into real tasks with energy levels and time estimates already filled in. nothing has to be organised before you write it down.

05 · your sky

a kindness streak, not a guilt streak

it counts the days you showed up, not the days you performed. miss one and it holds. you get a week of clouds, small trophies for things that deserve noticing, and a short reflection on what you actually did.

built for how adhd actually works

four things that make the day harder than it looks, and what cloudlist does about each.

01

task initiation paralysis

knowing what to do and being unable to start is not laziness, it's the gap between intention and action. splitting a task into steps closes that gap.

02

time blindness

half an hour and half a day feel the same from the inside. every task carries a time estimate, and focus mode runs a timer sized to the attention span you have.

03

out of sight, out of mind

a task you can't see may as well not exist. anything untouched for five days comes back on its own, without a telling-off.

04

overwhelm shutdown

twenty things reads as one impossible thing. cloudlist shows you what fits and quietly holds the rest until you're ready.

why cloudlist exists

hi, i'm germaine. i built cloudlist because nothing else worked for my adhd brain.

every task app assumed i'd remember to open it. every planner assumed i had linear focus. every productivity system assumed guilt and streaks would motivate me, and instead they crushed me on the days i couldn't keep up.

after years of trying to make other apps work, a friend told me to just build the thing i needed. so i did.

cloudlist is for people whose brains work like mine. warm, forgiving, quietly effective. made in melbourne.

questions

is it free?

the free version is properly usable: 10 active tasks, one break-it-down and one brain dump a day, seven days of history, and all three themes. pro removes the limits and adds focus sounds, priority flagging, full month history, and a weekly streak save. there's a seven day trial if you want to try it.

do i need a diagnosis?

no. cloudlist was built around adhd, but nothing about it requires a diagnosis. if the way it works makes sense to you, it's for you.

is there a focus timer?

yes. pick a task and focus mode runs a pomodoro sized to the attention span you set during onboarding. pro adds ambient sound.

is it on android?

not yet. iphone only for now.

what happens to what i type into the ai features?

break it down and brain dump send your text to anthropic's claude to generate a response, and nothing else. it isn't stored and isn't used for training. both ask for your consent during onboarding and can be turned off anytime in settings.

what if i stop using it for a while?

nothing bad happens. your tasks are still there, your streak is handled gently, and there's no guilt screen waiting for you.

can i cancel?

anytime, through your apple id settings. no flow designed to wear you down.
